Coaching Module · Worked Example

A Real Manager Coaching Conversation

This dialogue shows what good coaching sounds like — questions-first, never telling, every action owned by the coachee.

📋

Context: Sandra (Production Supervisor) is being coached by her manager on a recurring pattern — she keeps absorbing tasks her leads should own. This is their third session on the same theme. The goal for this session: help Sandra identify the exact moment she takes over, and build a concrete habit to stop it.

MGR

Manager

What's on your mind this week?
SAN

Sandra

I'm just stretched thin. I ended up doing the PM checklist audit myself instead of having Dave do it.
MGR

Manager

What's the gap between what happened and what you wanted to happen?
SAN

Sandra

I wanted him to own it. I just… didn't trust it would get done in time.
MGR

Manager

What's the exact moment you decided to take it back?
SAN

Sandra

Tuesday morning. He hadn't started yet, and I had 20 minutes before I needed the data.
MGR

Manager

What did you tell yourself in that moment?
SAN

Sandra

That it would just be faster if I did it.
MGR

Manager

What would it look like to catch that moment next time?
SAN

Sandra

I guess… checking in with him Monday, not Tuesday, so there's still time for him to fix it if he's behind.
MGR

Manager

What will you specifically do, by when?
SAN

Sandra

I'll check in with Dave every Monday at 9am on anything with a Wednesday deadline, instead of taking it over myself.
MGR

Manager

On a 1–10, how committed are you to that?
SAN

Sandra

Honestly, an 8.
MGR

Manager

What would make it a 10?
SAN

Sandra

Putting a recurring reminder in my calendar so it's not something I have to remember to remember.
MGR

Manager

Good — set that up today. How will I know it happened?
SAN

Sandra

I'll log it in the app after each Monday check-in.

🔑 What made this coaching effective

Questions only. The manager never told Sandra what to do — every insight and action came from her own thinking.

Pinpoint the moment. "What's the exact moment you decided to take it back?" forces precision instead of vague reflection.

Specific commitment. "Every Monday at 9am" is concrete and measurable — not "I'll try to delegate more."

1–10 commitment check. Anything below a 10 opens the door to remove the real barrier (the missing calendar reminder).

Accountability loop closed. "How will I know it happened?" anchors Sandra's commitment to a visible, logged action.
